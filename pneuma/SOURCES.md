| **Marciana** | Venice, Biblioteca Marciana, Gr. 172v — 13th-century codex, the earliest surviving text of the *Pneumatica*. Cited for the technical drawings. |
| **Schmidt** | W. Schmidt's Greek text of the *Pneumatica* (1899–1914), the standard critical edition. |

**A caution that must be carried through the whole lane:** Heron's *Pneumatica*
exists in two books (43 + 37 chapters) and **editions number its sections
differently** — Woodcroft numbers continuously, Schmidt numbers per book, and
some secondary sources misattribute "Book I, §50" to the aeolipile, which cannot
be right since Book I has only 43 chapters. Where a source disagrees, this file
cites Woodcroft's continuous number **and names the edition**, and the
disagreement is stated rather than smoothed over.

## A deviation the Director should know about

**The periaktoi are not Heron's.** The commission's §13.2 specifies scene changes
as "periaktoi rotations — three-sided prisms". The three-sided rotating prism
belongs to **Vitruvius**, *De architectura* V.6.8, and to the later Roman stage.
Heron's own scene-change machinery in *Automata* II is different, and better
documented: **painted-cloth backdrops drawn into position by cord and released by
triggers, plus the rolling sea panorama, plus doors that open and close.**

**Conservative assumption, logged per §36:** this lane builds Heron's actual
mechanism — cord-drawn backdrops, the rolling sea panorama, and the doors — and
treats the periaktoi as a *Vitruvian* device to be cited separately if wanted. A
rotating prism would have to be cited to Vitruvius, and building one in a Heron
machine would be an anachronism inside the artifact. Recorded here so the
reviewer does not have to find it.

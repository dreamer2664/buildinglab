# CONFLICTS.md — adjudications

Gate G2: where the record conflicts, the conflict is adjudicated here and the adjudication binds the generator. Nothing is silently averaged, and nothing is resolved by preference. Law: on board mechanics the agency epoch (`2026-10-20.3`) wins; on craft the master packet wins (packet header).

| id | conflict | sources | adjudication |
|---|---|---|---|
| **C-001** | Brick census: the packet floors the network at ≥61,000,000; the historical record gives 318,000,000 bricks for the whole main drainage | R-036, R-037 vs §13.2 | The 318 M figure covers 1,100 miles of street sewers, the four stations, the embankments and all ancillary works; the packet's 61 M is a **floor for the 1,320-chunk interceptor network only**. Recomputed geometry (C-002) yields 95.1 M for the network, which satisfies the floor. No number is reduced to meet an estimate. |
| **C-002** | Per-chunk density: packet says "~46,000 ±"; recomputation from the declared ovoid (2.700 × 1.350 m) and the 229 × 114 × 76 mm brick on a 2-wythe ring gives **72,044** per 100 m | §15.2 vs `tools/generator/profile.py` | §15.2 states the density is "recomputed from profile geometry, **not declared**". The recomputation wins: 72,044 per chunk, 95,098,080 network-wide. The ~46,000 estimate is recorded as an estimate and is not used anywhere in the pipeline. |
| **C-003** | Beam stroke: packet declares 3.05 m (10 ft); Crossness as built had a 9 ft (2.743 m) stroke | §15.3 vs R-064 | Packet wins on craft. Engines are modelled at 3.05 m; the historical 2.743 m is carried in `REFERENCES.md` and in each engine's `ENGINES.jsonl` row as `historical_stroke_m`, so no reader mistakes the model for the record. Δ = +0.307 m (+11.2 %). |
| **C-004** | Speed: packet declares 14 strokes/min; Crossness ran at 11 revolutions/min | §15.3 vs R-066 | A double-acting rotative engine gives two strokes per revolution, so 11 rev/min = 22 strokes/min and the packet's 14 strokes/min = **7.0 rev/min**. Both are recorded; the packet's value governs the animation and `v_cycle`. The engine's flywheel energy check is computed at 7.0 rev/min. |
| **C-005** | Abbey Mills cylinder: 54 in (1.372 m) at 142 hp; a contemporary report gives 64 in with a 9 ft stroke and a 40 ft beam; the 1865 tender specification called for eight 146 hp engines | R-085, R-087, R-086, R-088 | Adopt **54 in bore** and **146 hp** (the tender specification is the contractual document; the 64 in report is an outlier and is recorded). Pump cylinders 3 ft 10.5 in × 4.5 ft stroke per R-089, with R-090's 4 ft × 4.5 ft recorded as the variant. |
| **C-006** | Ring closure: the mid-ring circumference (7.104 m) is not an integer multiple of the 239 mm module | geometry | One **cut closure brick** per course closes the ring, exactly as site practice did; joint widths stay nominal 10 mm elsewhere in the course. The closure brick carries its own ledger row with `condition="cut"` and its own measured length. Closure length varies per course because the ring perimeter varies with the profile segment. |
| **C-007** | Crossness pumps: eight 4 ft 6 in (1.372 m) plungers in a 12 ft (3.66 m) casing; another report calls the plunger pumps 9 ft diameter and "the largest in the world" | R-071 vs R-072 | Adopt the specific description: 8 × 1.372 m plungers in a 3.66 m casing per engine. The 9 ft figure is recorded as the barrel/casing dimension reported by the contemporary guide. Lift 5.5 t per plunger per stroke to 9.1–9.8 m (R-073). |
| **C-008** | London Bridge tide levels: published predictions give HW 5.3 m and 5.6 m with LW 2.3 m and 1.7 m (range 3.0–3.9 m); the PLA booklet row carries 5.9 / 7.1 / 7.6 m; datums differ (Chart Datum ≈ LAT vs Ordnance Datum Newlyn) | R-113, R-114, R-115, R-118 | Harmonic site constants are **fitted by least squares to the published predicted levels** for London Bridge (Tower Pier, PLA gauge 0113) rather than assumed, and the fit residual is reported in `evidence/TIDE_FIT.json`. All model water levels are carried in metres above Chart Datum with the datum stated per row. The Admiralty constituent table for the site was not obtainable in this session; that is recorded as an open item (D-003) and the fitted constants are provisional against G7's ±0.15 m, which is evaluated against the same published predictions. |
| **C-009** | "Each brick: its own object" (§13.2) vs "chunks open standalone headless in <120 s" (§15.5) on a 2-core / 1.9 GB worker with no swap | §13.2, §15.5, agency `SANDBOX.md` | A 72,044-object chunk cannot be opened inside the declared gate on the declared worker; the two gates cannot both be satisfied literally, so the **craft intent** is preserved and the literal reading is adjudicated: a brick is an individually modelled closed 8-vertex island with its own ±2 mm deviation, its own ledger row and a position recomputed and verified from that row. Islands are carried in course-band objects (one per wythe per 100 courses). Census = island count from mesh connectivity. Measured proxy evidence: `evidence/CENSUS/`, `evidence/PROXY_TEST.md`. If measurement shows 72,044 individual objects do open in <120 s within 1.2 GB, the literal reading is restored and this row is superseded by a new row (R5). |
| **C-010** | Crossness engines were converted to triple expansion in 1895–1901 | R-081, R-082 | Out of period (1858–1875). All four are modelled in their **1865 single-cylinder double-acting** state: 1.219 m bore, cam-operated drop valves, 11 rev/min historical / 7.0 rev/min per C-004. The triple-expansion cylinders (19/32/44 in) are recorded in `REFERENCES.md` and never built. |
| **C-011** | Crossness rocking beams: "two great rocking beams weighing 28 tons each connect each pair of wheels" vs "main beams weighing 47 tons" vs "beams 43 ft long" vs "42 ft" | R-069, R-070, R-065, R-080 | Adopt 42–43 ft (12.80–13.11 m) long × 6 ft (1.83 m) deep, **two beams per engine at 28 t each**; the 47 t figure is recorded as the beam-assembly weight given by the trade press. Where a single engine's part schedule needs one number it carries 12.95 m (42 ft 6 in), the midpoint of the reported range, with both endpoints cited. |
| **C-012** | Network length: the packet declares 132.0 km over five lines; the record gives 82 miles (132 km) of intercepting sewers but also "six main interceptors, almost 100 miles (160 km)" | R-001, R-002 | 82 miles = 132.07 km and is the figure contemporaneous with Bazalgette's own scheme; the ~100 miles figure includes later additions outside 1858–1875. Adopt **132.0 km** across the five declared lines, and record the line-length allocation used (which the record does not give line by line) as a modelled allocation in `PROFILE.json` with its basis stated. |
| **C-013** | Weir crest levels for the six storm-relief overflows are not given in the record | no source | Derived, not chosen: **capacity** reliefs on the two high-level lines take a side weir at 0.75 × section height above the invert, so the sewer must genuinely surcharge before it spills; **tidal** reliefs on the three low/mid-level lines take a crest at Z0 + A(M2) + 0.15 m CD, between MHWN and MHWS, so a spring tide can reach it. Both operate on sim conditions only; a crest placed above the astronomical maximum would make the mechanism untestable, which is what the first run of this sim did (see D-007). |
| **C-014** | No hourly rainfall/runoff record for the five 1865 catchments was obtainable in this session | none available | The storm series is **modelled and declared as modelled**: a rain day about one time in three (London ~110 rain days per year), ordinary events peaking at 2-6x DWF, about one day in ten a major event peaking at 8-25x DWF, seeded and reproducible. The overflow logic consumes it; no line of the overflow code selects an outcome. Carried as open item D-007 until a period rainfall series is sourced. |
| **C-015** | The packet counts "all 1,319 joins"; five separate lines give 1,315 intra-line joins | §14/G3 vs `PROFILE.json` line schedule | 1,319 = 1,320 - 1 assumes one continuous chain. The network is five lines with their own heads and outfalls, so the joins that exist are **1,315** (300-1 + 270-1 + 220-1 + 280-1 + 250-1). All 1,315 are checked, all continuous to 0.000 mm (integer-millimetre arithmetic), zero uphill metres. The four line-to-station transitions are not joins and are modelled as station inlets instead. Both numbers are reported so a reviewer is never left reconciling them alone. |
| **C-016** | 100 m is not an integer multiple of the 86 mm course module, so a chunk boundary breaks the bond | geometry vs §13.1 (chunks of 100 m) | The ganger sets the gauge, as site practice did: courses = round(100 m / 86 mm) = **1,163** and the axial module becomes 100 m / 1,163 = **85.9845 mm**, moving each mortar joint by 0.0155 mm from nominal (well inside the ±2 mm deviation law and the ±10 mm join tolerance). Chainage stays exactly 100 m per chunk and 132.0 km across the network; the bond stays continuous across all 1,315 joins. Found by `v_joins`, which failed 9 of 9 adjacent pairs before the fix (D-010). Because the geometry rule changed, `GENERATOR_VERSION` went 1.0.0 -> 1.1.0 and **every key was re-cut**: reusing a 1.0.0 chunk would be F29. |
| **C-017** | Re-rendering a view appended a second row with the same id, so counts doubled | evidence law vs a render register | `evidence/RENDERS.jsonl` is a register, not a history: rows upsert by id, exactly as `DEVIATIONS.jsonl` upserts by chunk (D-009). The superseded render's sha256, time and reason stay inside the surviving row under `superseded`, so nothing is lost and no validator counts a view twice. History ledgers (DEFECTS, HEARTBEATS, VALIDATORS, WINDOW, GATES) stay append-only. |
| **C-018** | 1,320 chunk blends are 75 GB; the station has 18 GB and git history must never carry them | §1.6 chunk files vs disk and release law | The ledgers are the master and the blends are reproducible: `tools/reproduce.py` rebuilds a chunk clean-room to identical census and u_hash. The pipeline therefore keeps a rolling window of 40 blends and prunes the rest once the census is recorded; a pruned row is upserted to state `built-pruned` in INDEX.jsonl, and `v_index` requires the census record (not the binary) for such rows. Reviewers open pruned chunks by rebuilding them, which is what G13 asks for anyway. |
| **C-019** | `v_bricks` reading every built shard on each battery run costs minutes of CPU the chunk pipeline needs | §27 sampling law vs the 2 GB / chunked-work doctrine | The uniqueness and mesh-link samples rotate: each battery run audits up to 12 chunks it has not audited yet, and cumulative coverage is written to `evidence/BRICK_AUDIT.json` and reported in the verdict. Coverage grows monotonically; no chunk escapes the audit, the audit simply does not re-read what it has already measured. |
| **C-020** | Plain brick shards are git-ignored working copies, so an unpushed pipeline ledger existed only on one disk, and 9 GB of plain shards against 4.8 GB free would have halted the lay | R3 (only pushes survive) vs .gitignore vs disk | The gzipped shard is the master and is committed; the plain shard is a working copy deleted once its gzip verifies (sha256 of the decompressed gzip equals sha256 of the plain). `tools/compress_shards.sh` runs beside the pipeline and never touches the chunk being written. A gzip older than its plain is stale and regenerates, which also retires the 1.0.0 gzips beside 1.1.0 plains. S0042 keeps its plain ledger tracked as the reproduction exhibit. |
| **C-021** | Holding flywheel angular velocity within +/-2 % at the packet's 14 strokes/min needs more rim than the historical engines carried | G6 vs C-003 vs the historical record | Stored flywheel energy scales with the square of angular velocity. C-003 runs the engines at 7.0 rev/min against the historical 11, so the same rim stores 0.40 of the energy and the speed ripple grows by the inverse. The flywheel search in `tools/generator/kinematics.py` therefore takes rim mass as a design variable up to 4x the historical mass, at the recorded diameters, and reports the chosen mass, inertia, cut-off and measured deviation per engine in `evidence/FLYWHEEL_<id>.json`. The historical masses remain on record in REFERENCES.md; the heavier rims are a consequence of the commission's own stroke rate, not a drift from it. |
